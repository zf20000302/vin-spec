# Literature Review

Map a research area, choose useful papers, and compare their evidence
in a way that serves the user's current research and learning goal.

## Scope this round

Use existing context and ask only for missing information that changes
the work: question, purpose, boundaries, background, time, and desired
output. Allow the question to evolve as reading reveals better directions.

State whether this is an exploratory map, a focused comparison, or a
formal review. Match search effort and reporting to that purpose.

## Search and build the paper map

Use appropriate scholarly indexes and primary sources. Record search
dates, queries, coverage, filters, inclusion decisions, and access limits.

Organize papers by research question and relationship: overviews,
foundations, representative approaches, recent developments,
alternatives, extensions, relaxed assumptions, and conflicting evidence.

Explain the value of reading each candidate. Include relevant venues
and verify their scope and publication information from official sources.
Use citation counts with their source, date, and field or age context
as discovery clues.

Mark abstract-only judgments as preliminary. Obtain the relevant full
text before judging detailed methods, derivations, or claim support.

For a requested systematic review, define eligibility and screening
procedures, retain exclusions and counts, distinguish search records,
reports, and independent studies, and link overlapping reports or
versions. Report who actually screened and the limits of the process.
Use an appropriate current reporting standard when applicable.
Describe only the coverage and procedures actually achieved.

## Select and order reading

Consider relevance to the question, learning value, evidence and
limitations, and prerequisite mathematics, code, or data.

Code availability can affect practical scheduling while valuable
theoretical work remains eligible. Explain the shortlist and reading
order, and let the user choose the direction and deep-reading targets.

Use [learning-coach.md](learning-coach.md) for a cumulative learning route
and [paper-guide.md](paper-guide.md) for the selected paper walkthrough.

## Extract and compare consistently

For each paper, preserve identity and version, source locations,
problem, method, assumptions, mathematical results, validation,
results and costs, limitations, and relevance to the user's question.

Use three comparison layers:

1. **Dimension:** What matters, such as effectiveness, reliability,
   computational cost, or practical applicability.
2. **Metric:** Purpose, formula, units, direction, and aggregation.
3. **Reported conditions:** Data, task, baselines, hardware or resources,
   repetitions, duration, uncertainty, and result location.

Compare papers addressing the same question. Establish whether metric
definitions, data, conditions, and resource budgets are comparable
before interpreting numerical differences. Keep theoretical guarantees
with their assumptions and theorem locations distinct from experiments.

Use [scientific-critical-thinking.md](scientific-critical-thinking.md)
for reliability appraisal. Preserve author claims, Codex inferences,
and the user's explanation as separate contributions.

## Synthesize and check support

Organize findings around questions and themes. Explain agreement,
disagreement, trade-offs, and unresolved gaps with their conditions.

For substantive conclusions, retain the path from the claim through
the metric and conditions to the original passage, equation, figure,
or table. Mark support as supported, partial, pending, or inconsistent.
Label cross-paper inferences and preserve their scope.

Use [citation-management.md](citation-management.md) for bibliographic
identity, versions, and source-to-claim checks. A gap means what remains
unresolved within the documented search and evidence boundary.

## Deliver and keep cumulative records

Prefer a concise paper map or comparison table, key findings, open
questions, and proposed next reading or verification actions.

Keep project-level maps, choices, and next steps linked to stable paper,
concept, and derivation records. Keep the journal brief and linked.
Follow SKILL.md's logging and file-authorization rules.

Use [hypothesis-generation.md](hypothesis-generation.md) when the user
wants to turn the synthesis into research questions and candidate tests.
