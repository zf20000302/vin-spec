# Scientific Critical Thinking

Evaluate a specific scientific claim through its assumptions,
reasoning, validation, and scope.

## Fix the claim and evidence

Identify the exact claim, target system, comparison, conditions,
outcome, and source version. Distinguish definitions, theoretical
results, empirical observations, predictions, and causal explanations.

Locate the original passages, equations, figures, tables, and relevant
supplementary material. State what was actually inspected and what
remains unavailable. Use [citation-management.md](citation-management.md)
when source identity or claim support needs checking.

## Examine the reasoning chain

- **Assumptions:** Are they explicit, justified, internally consistent,
  and applicable to the intended setting?
- **Derivation:** Check notation, domains, units, operations, theorems,
  added premises, approximations, and omitted steps. Separate the
  author's argument from supplementary reasoning.
- **Validation:** Does the design test the stated claim? Examine
  baselines, resource fairness, measurements, independent units,
  uncertainty, dependence, failures, and relevant data leakage or
  selection issues.
- **Scope:** What follows under the stated conditions, and what needs
  additional evidence before transfer to another setting?

A valid conditional theorem and its applicability to the user's system
are separate questions. Numerical examples can reveal issues, while a
general mathematical assertion needs an appropriate proof or
counterexample.

Distinguish a learner's missing prerequisite, ambiguous extraction,
missing reporting, and a demonstrated problem in the paper.
Support an alleged error with checkable reasoning or a counterexample.

## Match the appraisal to the field

Consider effect size and uncertainty, experimental units, sampling,
confounding, multiplicity, robustness, and exploratory versus planned
analysis when relevant. Predictive success or model fit alone does not
establish a causal explanation.

Use domain appraisal frameworks only when they fit the question and
unit of evidence. Verify their current versions if used. Keep reporting
completeness, risk of bias, and certainty across evidence separate.
Use reasoned judgments rather than a generic paper-quality score.

## Give a traceable judgment

Record each material concern through:

- Original location and affected claim.
- Observation and supporting evidence.
- Consequence for the claim or applicability.
- Current status and uncertainty.
- A feasible next check or remedy.

Report strengths, consequential concerns, unresolved questions, and
what can reasonably be concluded. Missing information stays pending
until evidence resolves it. Keep contrary and null findings visible.

Use [hypothesis-generation.md](hypothesis-generation.md) when a concern
leads to a new prediction or discriminating test.

## Keep learning and records distinct

Use [paper-guide.md](paper-guide.md) for the user's explanations,
questions, and independent whiteboard-derivation assessment.
Personal learning attainment and scientific reliability have separate
evidence records.

Keep detailed appraisal with the stable paper or derivation record,
link its implications from the project, and record dated decisions in
the journal. Follow SKILL.md's logging and file-authorization rules.
