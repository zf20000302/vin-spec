# Citation Management

Maintain accurate source identities, versions, references, and links
between claims and the evidence that supports them.

## Resolve identity and version

Use the identifiers and context supplied: DOI, publisher URL, title,
authors, year, venue, or preprint identifier. Verify against primary
publisher or repository records and appropriate bibliographic metadata.

Reconcile conflicting fields against the actual source. Distinguish
online-first and final publication states, article numbers, and fields
that are missing or not applicable. Keep unresolved fields explicit
rather than inventing metadata. Record the verification date, sources
consulted, and unresolved fields.

Link preprint, published, corrected, and other relevant versions.
Use the version supporting the actual cited passage. Check relevant
publisher correction or retraction information when assessing validity,
and record unresolved checks.

A resolving DOI or matching title is one identity check; verify that
the full record is the intended work.

## Check source-to-claim support

Separate bibliographic verification from substantive support.

For a claim, record the source version, original location, relevant
measurement or theoretical conditions, what the source establishes,
and the support status: supported, partial, pending, or inconsistent.

Inspect the relevant full text for detailed methods or conclusions.
Mark abstract-only or unavailable evidence accordingly. Preserve scope
and distinguish author statements from cross-source inferences.

Use [scientific-critical-thinking.md](scientific-critical-thinking.md)
when the question concerns reliability of the evidence itself.

## Maintain references

Reuse stable paper identities and consistent citation keys. Confirm
duplicates using bibliographic identity and identifiers while preserving
version relationships and the user's notes.

Produce the requested citation style or BibTeX fields from verified
metadata. Preserve author names, special characters, actual publication
state, and identifiers. Investigate missing or contradictory required
fields using suitable primary sources.

When editing a bibliography or manuscript, check unresolved keys,
duplicate records, metadata consistency, formatting, and correspondence
between in-text citations and bibliography entries. Use available
tools appropriate to the project and report checks that could not run.

## Scope and cumulative records

Use [literature-review.md](literature-review.md) for a broader discovery
or synthesis task. A citation lookup does not require rebuilding the
literature review.

Keep source identity, versions, locations, and verification basis in
the stable paper record. Keep project bibliography links and unresolved
citation tasks in the project record, with short dated journal entries.

Create or modify bibliography artifacts within the user's requested
scope. Follow SKILL.md's logging and file-authorization rules.
